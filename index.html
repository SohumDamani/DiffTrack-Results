DiffTrack —
Parameter Study & Limitation Analysis

Can a video diffusion model track points without any training labels?

Reproducing and stress-testing DiffTrack (NeurIPS 2025) — zero-shot point tracking via emergent query-key correspondences in a frozen CogVideoX-2B Video DiT. 13 experiments on a 4-video subset of TAP-Vid DAVIS (30 real-world video sequences). All results verified against raw run logs.

Setup

Dataset & Evaluation Protocol

All experiments use the TAP-Vid DAVIS benchmark — 30 densely annotated real-world video sequences covering a range of objects, camera motions, and occlusion patterns. Due to compute constraints, each ablation run used a fixed 4-video subset drawn from the 30 available. We ran two separate 4-video evaluations: one for the main parameter sweep, and one full-eval pass on a fresh subset to cross-check the baseline.

The primary metric is delta_avg — the average of five distance-threshold accuracies: what fraction of tracked points land within 1, 2, 4, 8, and 16 pixels of ground truth. Higher is better. delta_1 (within 1 pixel) is the strictest; delta_16 (within 16 pixels) is the most lenient. A method with high delta_16 but low delta_1 is accurate at a coarse scale but imprecise at sub-pixel level. The paper reports 46.3 on the full 30-video set; our 4-video runs came in at 47.0 (param study subset) and 49.4 (full-eval subset) — both within the expected variance for a 4-video sample.

Part A

Parameter Study: Strengths

Systematic ablation over two key parameters: which transformer layer to extract attention from, and which diffusion timestep to inject noise at. All runs use CogVideoX-2B on the TAP-Vid DAVIS 4-video subset. Videos below show predicted point trajectories (colored dots) overlaid on real footage.

Experiment 1 — Attention Layer Selection

CogVideoX-2B has 42 transformer layers. We tested five representative layers — shallow (5), early-mid (8), mid (17), deep (27), near-final (29) — at a fixed timestep of ts=49 across 4 DAVIS videos. The intuition: shallow layers capture low-level spatial detail with little semantic understanding; deep layers have rich semantics but may have lost spatial resolution. We expected mid-network layers to offer the best balance, and that is what we observed.

Mid-network layers balance spatial precision with semantic structure. Shallow layers lack temporal feature abstraction; deep layers over-smooth spatial detail. Layer 17 confirmed as the paper's best recommendation across all 5 layers tested.

Experiment 2 — Diffusion Timestep Selection

DiffTrack works by injecting Gaussian noise at a chosen timestep, then running one denoising step and reading attention maps. The timestep controls how much noise is added: high noise (small ts index, large t value) adds a lot; low noise (ts=49, t≈20) adds almost none. We swept six timesteps at layer=17 to see how tracking quality changes across the noise schedule. The key question: is there a minimum noise level needed for cross-frame correspondence to emerge?

Experiment 3 — Temporal Chunk Frame Interval

The paper uses a chunk_frame_interval heuristic that splits long videos into overlapping windows for attention extraction. The idea is to prevent the attention from having to span too many frames at once. We tested whether removing this windowing changes tracking quality on our 4-video subset.

A 0.6-point gap is within small-sample noise. The windowing heuristic cannot fix the fundamental lack of temporal memory — each frame is independently matched against frame 0 regardless of chunking strategy.

Part B

Limitations Analysis

The paper itself identifies several failure modes in the appendix. We ran targeted experiments to observe these directly. Each limitation below is paired with a video comparison and raw log data — the numbers make the failure mode concrete.

Limitation 1
Without noise, correspondence collapses — zero across all 4 videos

The pipeline feeds clean VAE latents to a transformer conditioned at t=980 (near-maximum noise). Distribution mismatch destroys all feature correspondences. Correspondence is emergent from denoising — it was never explicitly trained. Remove the noise and there is nothing to track.

Limitation 2 — Layer Sensitivity (paper Figures 6 & A.18)

Our layer ablation (Experiment 1) showed a 16-point spread from best to worst layer. Two specific failure modes are worth zooming in on. Layer 8 tracks positions rather than objects — the RoPE positional embeddings dominate its Q-K scores, so the matched location in each frame is close to where the query point started at frame 0, not where the object moved. Layer 29 produces diffuse, scattered attention — no concentrated correspondence peak. Both behaviors are noted in the paper (Fig 6 and Fig A.18), and we observe them directly.

Limitation 2
Performance spans 16+ points across layers — with no selection criterion

Layer 8 matches objects at the same spatial position (RoPE positional embeddings dominate, paper Figure 6). Layer 29 shows diffuse, scattered attention (paper Figure A.18). The paper provides no empirical guide for layer selection on a new domain or architecture.

Limitation 3 — Precision vs Coarse Accuracy: A 12× Gap

DiffTrack matches each frame independently against frame 0 — there is no motion model, no velocity estimate, no memory of where the point was in the previous frame. This shows up clearly when you look at the accuracy across different distance thresholds. The method does reasonably well at coarse tolerances (within 8–16 pixels), but struggles at fine precision (within 1–2 pixels). The numbers below are from our best configuration (Layer 17, ts=49) on the param-study 4-video set.

Limitation 3
12× accuracy spread — no motion model, no trajectory history

Only 7% of predictions land within 1 pixel vs 81% within 16 pixels. The method independently matches each frame against frame 0 with no velocity estimate or trajectory history. Without temporal continuity, drift compounds over long sequences — the method is a one-shot frame matcher, not a tracker in the classical sense.

Part C

CAG Motion Guidance Demo

Cross-Frame Attention Guidance (CAG) repurposes the same cross-frame query-key attention that enables tracking — now for motion transfer between two generated videos.

During denoising, the cross-frame keys and values from a reference video are injected into the generation of a target video. Because these attention maps encode temporal correspondence, the target video's motion pattern is steered toward the reference — zero-shot, same frozen model, no training. The two videos below show a generation with no injection vs one with CAG applied at PAG strength=1.

PAG Strength = 0 — No Guidance
Baseline generation with no cross-frame attention injection. Video DiT generates plausible but uncontrolled motion — no source trajectory is applied.
PAG Strength = 1 — With CAG Applied
During denoising, cross-frame keys/values from a reference video replace those in the target. This injects the reference motion pattern — same attention maps that track points now transfer motion.
Key Insight
Same attention maps — two applications: tracking and motion transfer

The cross-frame query-key attention that enables zero-shot point tracking also enables zero-shot motion transfer. CAG demonstrates the generality of the correspondence mechanism — it is a fundamental property of how video DiTs organize temporal information, not a tracking-specific artifact.

Root Causes Identified

Root Cause 1 — Noise Dependency
Correspondence is emergent, not designed

Tracking works because denoising at ts=30–49 creates temporally-coherent attention maps as a side effect. The model was never explicitly trained to track — too little noise (ts≤5) collapses the mechanism entirely.

Root Cause 2 — No Temporal Memory
Frame-0 independent matching only

Every frame is matched against frame 0 independently — no velocity estimate, no motion prior, no trajectory history. Precision degrades 12× from 1px to 16px threshold with no way to recover accumulated drift.

New Findings Beyond the Paper

New Findings
What we add beyond the paper's analysis

Plateau framing: ts=30–49 form an effective operating plateau — the 0.8-point difference is within small-sample noise, not a meaningful peak.

Hard noise floor: A sharp failure cliff at ts≤5 — ts=10 gives 13.1, ts=5 gives 0.8. Below this floor the tracker fails as completely as at ts=1.

Threshold-accuracy spread: The 12× gap between delta_1 and delta_16 is not discussed in the paper; it reveals the method's fundamental precision ceiling for applications requiring sub-pixel accuracy.
